# The case for F: Echo

*(A few words you will meet: the **D-pad** is the remote's ring of Up / Down / Left / Right around OK. The **focus**
is the one thing on screen the remote is "holding"; the **focus ring** is the white outline that shows it. A
**backdrop** is the wide picture a show or film has besides its poster. A **leaf** is my word for one of the tall
panels Home is made of; a **spine** is a leaf when it is folded shut, like the edge of a book on a shelf. The
**shield** is the spoiler shield: scores and results you have not seen are kept off the screen.)*

## 1. In one breath

Home is six tall leaves standing side by side across the whole screen, like a folding screen or a row of books.
One leaf is open: it is a big square of the show's real picture, with its logo, one line that says where you are, and
one button. The other five stand folded beside it as spines, each a strip of its own artwork with its name running up
the edge. Press Right and the next leaf opens where it stands while this one folds; the six things that matter
tonight are always all on screen, and the one you are on is the size of the screen.

## 2. An evening, press by press

**20:40, he turns on the TV, mid-Severance.** Home opens with the first leaf open: Severance's picture fills the
left 540 px top to bottom, `CONTINUE WATCHING`, the Severance logo, `S2 · E3 · Who Is Alive? · 21 min left · Ends
21:03 · 4K`, the episode's line, the progress bar, and the pink `▶ Resume` button already holding the focus. To the
right, five spines: Slow Horses (a pink dot: new episode), Blade Runner 2049 (`ends 23:27 · 4K`), Grizzlies at
Blazers (`Thu 02:00`, navy with both crests), Kastljós (`RÚV · on now`), Library (`4,812 films · 1,210 series`).

- **Resume the show: 1 press** (OK). Nothing to find, nothing to move.
- Episodes: Down (the leaf opens into Your shows, Severance's episodes first), then OK. Start over: Hold OK.
- **Watch the next episode of Slow Horses: 2 presses** (Right opens its leaf, OK plays).

**Game night, 03:12, Grizzlies at Lakers, shield on.** Home opens with the game as the open leaf: the Memphis navy
field and the Lakers purple field cut by the slanted line, the two crests big, `Grizzlies · 3-0 · as of tip-off`,
`Lakers · 2-2`, the clock chip `2ND 4:12`, a `Score hidden` plate, and on the dark band under them `GAME NIGHT ·
LIVE`, `Grizzlies at Lakers`, `2nd quarter · ESPN · 4K`, the fan line, and `▶ Watch live` holding the focus. The NBA
tab in the top bar wears the LIVE pill. The spines beside it: Severance, Slow Horses, It's Always Sunny (`short · 25
min`), Nuggets at Thunder (`also live · TNT`), Library.

- **Watch the game live: 1 press** (OK).
- From tip-off: Down (the game's own shelf: From tip-off first, then the game page and the other games), OK: 2.
- Reveal the score: Hold OK, then confirm. Never by accident.
- When the game is over, he presses Right once and Severance is the big picture again.

**He wants a film he hasn't picked** (a normal evening). From the Severance screen: Right, Right. The third leaf
opens in the middle of the row (that is the fourth mock, `sideways`): Blade Runner 2049's picture, `A FILM FOR
TONIGHT · ENDS BEFORE MIDNIGHT`, the logo, `2017 · 2h 44m · Ends 23:27 · 4K · HDR · IMDb 8.0`, two lines of plot,
`▶ Play` holding the focus.

- **Play the app's pick: 3 presses** (Right, Right, OK).
- **Browse instead:** one more Down. Tonight folds into a thin strip of six chips at the top (so he never loses it),
  and below it the library's shelves stand as leaves: `A film for tonight · 212 · ends before midnight` open, with
  Blade Runner 2049's picture and a row of posters (Blade Runner, Sinners, Dune: Part Two, Heat, Conclave), the first
  poster ringed. Right moves along the posters and the big picture and logo change with each; off the end of the
  row, the next shelf (Popular on Cinemeta) opens. OK is Details, Hold OK is Play now. Up or Back unfolds tonight.
  **A film he picks himself: 3 presses to the shelf, then one press per poster.**
- **A channel: 5 presses** (Right four times to Kastljós, OK tunes). Or the Live tab in the bar as everywhere.

Everything he does most is one press (resume, the game), and the two next things (a new episode, the film the app
chose for tonight) are two or three.

## 3. Why it works

**Reading distance.** From three metres a 55" screen is about the size of a magazine held at arm's length. What you
can read at that distance is a logo, a face, a name and one line; what you cannot read is a grid of forty captions.
So Echo puts six things on screen and gives each of them a picture and a name, and gives the one you are on the
whole left half: the logo at 54 px (108 on the panel), the facts at 15 px, the button at 44 px high. The spines'
names are Outfit 700 at 22 px (44 on the panel), bigger than most apps' row titles.

**One focus at a time.** On a remote the eye follows the focus, and the focus must be unmistakable. In Echo the
focus is never ambiguous: the open leaf is half the screen, and inside it a single pink button wears the white ring.
Left and Right do exactly one thing (open the next leaf), Down does exactly one thing (walk through the leaf), OK does
the leaf's one obvious action. There is no "which row am I in", no "did my Right go to the next card or the next
row".

**How many choices.** Six. That is what fits as pictures a person can tell apart from the sofa, and it is the plan
for tonight: what you are in the middle of, what is new in your shows, a film that ends before bed, the game, what is
on now, and the door to everything else. The owner's rule from the earlier rounds was "a short readable plan for
tonight"; Echo keeps it to six and never adds a seventh. Everything else is one press Down.

**What the eye lands on first.** The open leaf's picture, then its logo, then the pink button. The spines are
veiled (a dark gradient over them, heavier at the bottom where the names are) and each one a step darker than the
last away from the open leaf, so the open leaf is the only bright thing. That stepping is also the brand: the open
leaf and the spines fading beside it are the airglow mark's wedge and its echo bands, drawn with tonight's pictures.
That is where the name comes from.

**Muscle memory.** The leaves have a fixed order: continue, new, a film, the game, on now, library. After a week
his thumb knows Right-Right is the film and Right-Right-Right-Right is the channel, and that Down from anything is
"more of this". The order changes only between visits, never while he is looking; an empty leaf is left out and the
rest get wider, but nothing is ever shuffled.

**4,812 films and 1,210 series.** The six leaves are not the library; they are the doors to it, and the library uses
the same idea one level down. Down through any leaf gives a strip of tonight at the top and the shelves standing as
leaves below: the shelf you walked into is open and shows its posters, the others are spines (`Popular on Cinemeta`,
`New this month`, `Á íslensku · 38 films · 6 series`, `In 4K · 412`, `Saved · 12`), with their counts on the spine.
A shelf is a normal poster row inside its leaf; running off its end opens the next shelf. So the whole library is
reachable with Down, Right and OK, and he never meets a second kind of page: the library looks like Home, only one
level down. The Library leaf itself opens on the doors (everything, by genre, by decade, by language).

**What the others do.** Netflix, Disney+, Max and Google TV are a billboard over rows of thumbnails: one big thing,
then forty small ones, and the big one changes by itself. Apple TV is the same shape, quieter and better art. Plex and
Infuse are rows with better posters. They all share the problem the owner put his finger on: the first screen is
either one thing or forty, never "the six that matter, all as pictures". Echo deliberately drops rows from the first
screen. It keeps what makes Netflix feel expensive (one big real picture, the show's own logo, a single clear button)
and trades the rows for leaves. Where it is deliberately different: nothing on Home scrolls, nothing autoplays or
rotates, every leaf says why it is there in its spine, and the picture is tall, not wide, because a tall picture is
what lets six of them share one screen.

## 4. Why it's good UI, piece by piece

**The leaves, full-bleed.** The six leaves run from the top of the screen to the bottom and from edge to edge, so
Home is made of pictures, not of boxes on a background. The text keeps to the safe area (the part of the screen every
TV shows, 48 px in from the sides, 27 from top and bottom). Full-bleed is what makes it feel like the TV *is* the
picture, the way a Netflix billboard does.

**The open leaf: 540 × 540.** Half the width, the whole height. Big enough that a face is life-size on a 55", small
enough that five spines fit beside it at 84 px each (6 × 84 = 504; 540 + 420 = 960). The picture is the show's
backdrop cropped to a square with its subject kept (Severance's man with the office on his head, Gosling's face). The
words sit at the bottom left over a dark gradient (a scrim: a fade from clear to the canvas colour, so chalk text is
readable on any picture), in the order you read them: what kind of thing this is (`CONTINUE WATCHING`, in the brand's
pink), the logo, the facts, the line, the progress, the button.

**The logo instead of a typed name.** Cinemeta has the real logo of nearly every title; using it is what makes the
leaf look like the show and not like the app. Severance's logo is black, so it is drawn in chalk (the warm off-white
the brand uses for text). Blade Runner's is red chrome and is used as it is. When there is no logo, the name is set
in Outfit 700 at 38 px, as the game's is.

**The one button.** The brand's pill, pink to orchid, with the play wedge and one verb: `Resume`, `Play`, `Watch
live`, `Tune`. It is the only gradient on the screen besides the progress bar and the clock chip, so it is where the
eye goes. It wears the focus ring (5 px white with a 2 px gap of canvas, the app's rule everywhere). The other
actions are quiet key hints beside it (`▼ Episodes`, `Hold Start over, Details`) so he learns the keys from the
screen itself, and they never compete with the button.

**The spines: 84 px, named along the edge.** Each spine is a strip of its own artwork, the strip chosen around the
subject (Gary Oldman's face, Gosling's), so a spine still looks like its show. The name runs up the spine, bottom to
top, in Outfit 700 22 px, with one fact beside it in small caps (`NEW · S6 · E6`, `FILM · ENDS 23:27 · 4K`). A
book's spine is the oldest way of naming something in a row of tall things, and it keeps the picture whole. Three
spines are not pictures, by rule: the game is its team's navy with the two crests (no programme art exists for a
game), the channel is the provider's logo on a flat field (no programme art exists for a channel), and the Library is
a little stack of four posters with its count.

**The veil and the step.** Every spine carries a dark gradient (light at the top, heavy at the bottom where the name
sits) so chalk text is always readable on any picture; and each spine further from the open leaf is a step darker
(0, 10, 18, 26, 32 %). Together they make one bright thing and a row of quieter ones: calm, with depth.

**Colour.** The canvas and surfaces are airglow's dark violet-ink; text is chalk; pink and orchid are only on the
button, the progress bar, the clock chip, the eyebrow word and the "new" dot. Team colours are the teams' real
colours and appear only on the game leaf and the game spines, as data, never on a button, a ring or a pill. The LIVE
pill is red with the word, the only red on the screen.

**Type.** Outfit 700 for names and spines (the brand's display face), Inter for everything you read (facts, lines,
hints), Barlow Condensed 800 italic only for the clock, as the brand allows for the live dialect. Facts are 15 px
(30 on the panel), lines 16, hints 14, the floor the brand set for text on this TV.

**Motion.** One movement: the fold. Six boxes change their left edge and width over 320 ms with the app's ease, the
pictures stay put and are clipped (they never scale or slide), and the opening leaf's words fade in after. The
previous leaf's name turns into a spine as it folds. No autoplay, no video, no parallax. It is the kind of motion a
person presses Right and Left to watch again.

**The top bar.** Unchanged from the rest of the app: the mark, the tabs, the clock, the account. It sits over a top
scrim so it reads over any leaf. On game night the NBA tab wears the LIVE pill, as it does everywhere in the app.

**The library leaves.** The same parts one level down: the strip of six chips at the top (the one you came through is
outlined in pink; `▲ Tonight` takes you back), the open shelf leaf with the focused film's backdrop, logo, facts and
plot, and its posters at the bottom with the ring on one; the other shelves as spines with a poster strip and their
count. Posters carry only the copy's quality badge (4K, HD); the time it would end is in the facts above, so the row
stays clean.

## 5. Game night

**Why a leaf and not a takeover.** On game night the game takes the open leaf and first place, so the first thing he
sees at 03:12 is the two teams, and `Watch live` is one press. But the evening does not disappear: Severance, Slow
Horses, a short episode and the other live game are standing beside it in the same places as always, so when the
game is over (or he is not in the mood) the rest of Home is one press away and looks exactly as it did last night. A
mode he can see, not a different app.

**Why it looks the way it does.** The leaf's top two thirds are the two teams' real colours cut by the brand's one
slanted line, with the two crests in neutral discs and the names under them, as the NBA pages already draw them. The
bottom third is a dark band with the words and the button: the team colours never touch the LIVE pill or the focus
ring (a brand rule, and a readability one), and no sentence ever crosses the slanted cut. The clock chip and the
`Score hidden` plate sit on the edge between the two, half on the colours, half on the band: the one place both
belong.

**How the shield keeps him safe.** Nothing on the leaf is a result. The records are stamped `as of tip-off`, so they
are what he could have known before the game started. The chip shows the quarter and the clock (which the app's rules
allow: they are time, not a result) and the plate says `Score hidden` in words, with the eye-off icon, so he knows the
shield is working rather than wondering whether the score is simply missing. The fan line (`3rd in the West · 3 in a
row · Morant 27.8 ppg`) is the copy kept at tip-off, with `as of tip-off` printed on it, so a streak can never give
away tonight. The other live game's spine says `Also live · TNT` and no more. Reveal is behind Hold OK and a confirm,
never on a key he can press by accident. And the strip of tonight in the library screen shows the game as a chip with
the crest, no numbers.

## 6. The weak spots, honestly

**Tall crops.** The biggest one. Every backdrop is a wide picture, and the open leaf cuts a square out of it. A face
in the middle (Severance, Blade Runner) looks wonderful; a wide landscape or a group with people at both edges loses
its sides. Cinemeta gives no focal point. The fix: a cheap face/contrast scan of the small 32 px copy the app already
fetches for ambient colour, used to set where the crop sits; and when a picture has its subject at an edge, the leaf
falls back to the poster's colour as a flat ground with the logo, which still looks like the show. A's one wide
picture does not have this problem, and that is A's real advantage.

**Sideways names.** Reading a spine means tilting the eye. Outfit 700 at 22 px sideways reads from the sofa (it is
44 px on the panel), but a long name is cramped (`Grizzlies at Trail Blazers` becomes `Grizzlies at Blazers`), and the
small line beside it is 13 px, one below the brand's 14 px floor for text. The fix is copy, not layout: names capped
at 20 characters, the small line at 14 px and under 20 characters. If after a month the sideways names still annoy
him, the spines can show a horizontal label at the bottom instead (a 2-line name in a dark plate); the fold is the
idea, not the sideways text.

**Only six on screen.** A person who wants to see "all my shows" at once will not get it on the first screen; it is
one Down away (Your shows, as a shelf). I think six is right for a plan for tonight, but it is the thing to test on the
real TV: if he presses Down every time, the first screen is doing too little.

**Faces in a row.** Six pictures side by side can turn into a strip of faces. The veil and the step-back hold it in
the mocks; the rule for the app is that a spine whose art fights its name (a bright, busy strip) falls back to the
poster's colour. The game, channel and Library spines are already flat by rule, which breaks the row up.

**The fold on the Philips.** The WebView (the browser inside the app) on this TV is a 2023 Chrome; animating the
width of six boxes re-lays out each frame. That is cheap when the boxes are six, the pictures are not scaled (only
clipped), nothing has a blur or a shadow, and the text is swapped after the fold, not during. I would build it as
`left` and `width` transitions first and measure on the TV; if it drops frames, the fallback is a cut with a 200 ms
fade of the two leaves that change, which still reads as the leaf opening. There is no video, no blur, no parallax
anywhere in the design.

**Image loading.** Six pictures at open size (540 × 540 at 2× = about 1080 px wide) per visit: the open leaf's and
the next leaf's load first, the spines' after (they show a flat colour and their name until then); the images are the
metahub `medium` size the app already uses, cached by the WebView. The library screen adds one backdrop and five
posters at a time, loaded as the focus moves, never a shelf ahead. The clock on the game leaf is one text node
updated each second; nothing else redraws on a tick.

**After a month.** What could wear thin is the fold itself if it is slow, and the sideways names if they are long.
What should not wear thin is the shape: six things, one big, Right to look, OK to play.

## 7. Versus the other two

**A, Marquee.** A is one full-screen picture and a list of six names down the left; the focused name becomes the
show's logo. *Where A wins:* the picture is the full 16:9 backdrop, uncropped, which is the most cinematic frame of
the three and the safest with any art; the six names are horizontal and fastest to read; and it is the least new code
(a list and a picture). *Where Echo wins:* A shows one picture and five words, Echo shows six pictures and still gives
the chosen one half the screen, so the plan reads as artwork and the things he is not on are still things he can see;
A's game night replaces the whole screen with the team colours and the rest of the evening goes grey beside it, where
Echo's game is a leaf with the evening standing in colour beside it; and A has two layouts (the list, then a poster
shelf on Down) where Echo has one idea at both levels. Both put resume and the game at one press and the film at three
or four.

**D, Reels.** D is four tall plates, one open, three as spines, and Up / Down spins each plate through its own list
(`3 / 212`), with the picture's colour glowing in the room behind. *Where D wins:* the spin means the library never
leaves Home (browsing a film is the same plate getting a different film, with a counter), which is a clever two-axis
idea; the spines are wider (124 px) with the real logo horizontal, which is easier to read than Echo's sideways names;
and the glow in the room is a nice use of the brand's name. *Where Echo wins:* D's four reels are kinds ("Pick up",
"New", "A film", "Live"), so the channel is hidden behind the game in the Live reel and the Library is nowhere on the
first screen, while Echo's six leaves are the actual things (the show, the episode, the film, the game, the channel,
the library) and each spine names its own; D asks him to learn two axes (which reel, then spin) and a reel's first
item is the only thing he sees of that kind until he spins, where Echo's Left / Right is the only move on the first
screen and Down is always "more of this"; D's plates are boxes on a glowing background, Echo's leaves are the whole
screen, full-bleed, which is closer to the "Netflix quality" he asked for; and D's room glow is a full-screen blur,
which D's own notes say must be faked on this TV, while Echo has no blur at all. On the hard problem both share, tall
crops of wide art, D has the same weakness, and D's spines being wider helps it a little.

**What I would say to the reviewer.** A is the safest and the most cinematic frame; D is the cleverest browsing; Echo is
the one where the first screen is the whole evening as pictures and the chosen one is the size of the screen, with one
move to learn and the brand's own shape as the layout. If the tall crop is solved the way section 6 says, it is the
one I would want on the TV.
