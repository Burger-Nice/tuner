# The case for A: Marquee

The three mocks this is about: `png/evening.png`, `png/gamenight.png`, `png/browse.png`. The annotated copy
`png/case-evening-annotated.png` numbers the pieces that section 4 walks through.

## 1. In one breath

Home is a cinema marquee for one person. Down the left, in big letters, is tonight's lineup: the show you are in the
middle of, what is new in your shows, the game, a film that fits before bed, what is on RÚV right now. Whatever you
point at owns the whole screen: the title's real artwork fills the picture, its real logo lights up where its name
was, one line says where you are and when it ends, and OK plays it.

## 2. An evening, press by press

**20:40, mid-Severance.** The TV comes on. Home opens with the lineup and the first line is already lit: *Continue
watching · Severance · S2 · E3 · Who Is Alive? · 21 min left · Ends 21:03 · 4K*, the episode's own sentence under it,
the progress bar, and the office with the desk on Mark's head filling the screen behind. Under it, dimmer: *Slow
Horses · New this week · S6 · E6*, *Breaking Bad · Up next · S3 · E7*, *Heat · A film for tonight · ends 23:33 · 4K*,
*Grizzlies at Trail Blazers · Thu 02:00*, *Kastljós · On now · RÚV · until 20:55*. Bottom right: *↓ Library · 4,812
films · 1,210 series*. He presses **OK**. Severance resumes. **One press.**

If he wants the next episode of Slow Horses instead: **Down, OK**: two presses. The picture changed to Slow Horses on
the Down, so he saw what he was choosing before he chose it.

**Game night, 03:12 (a late tip-off, as they are in Iceland).** The TV comes on. The screen is the two teams:
Memphis navy on the left, Lakers purple on the right, cut by one slanted line; the two crests big; the clock chip
*2ND 4:12 · synced* sitting on the cut; beside it a dark plate: *Score hidden*. The first line of the lineup is the
game, lit, on a slanted plate: *GAME NIGHT · LIVE · Grizzlies at Lakers · 2nd quarter · ESPN · 4K · Score hidden*,
and the fan line as of tip-off. He presses **OK**: the game, live. **One press.** Wants it from tip-off instead:
**Right, OK**. Wants to see the score: **Hold OK, Reveal**, then a one-step confirm so a thumb can never do it by
accident. Everything else from his evening (Severance, Slow Horses, a 25-minute Always Sunny, the other live game) is
still there under the game line, one Down each.

**A film he has not picked.** From the top of the lineup: **Down, Down, Down** lands on *Heat · A film for tonight ·
ends 23:33 · 4K* with Heat's artwork behind it; **OK** plays it: **four presses** to a film the app chose because it
ends before midnight and he has a 4K copy. If he wants to choose himself: on the Heat line press **Right** (or keep
going Down past the last line): the lineup leaves upward and the shelf *A film for tonight · 212* appears along the
bottom, big posters, each with its copy's quality and the time it would end; the one he is on (Sinners) puts its own
artwork, logo, year, length, rating, source and two lines of plot on the picture. **Left / Right** walk the shelf;
**Down** goes to the next shelf (the list at the right names them: *Popular on Cinemeta, New this month, Á íslensku,
In 4K, Saved, Everything · 4,812*); **OK** opens the title page, **Hold OK** plays now or saves. **Back** or **Up** from
the first shelf brings the lineup back.

**A channel.** *Kastljós · On now · RÚV* is the last line: **Down ×5, OK** tunes it (six presses), or **Right** on it
opens the guide at RÚV. The Live tab is still there: **Up, Right, Right, OK** from the top line.

| What he does most | Presses |
|---|---|
| Resume the show he is in | 1 |
| Watch the game live on game night | 1 (from tip-off: 2) |
| Play the film the app picked for tonight | 4 |
| Start choosing a film himself (the shelf, with pictures) | 4 |
| Tune the channel that is on now | 6 (or the Live tab in 4) |
| Any of the six things on screen | at most 6 |

## 3. Why it works

**You read names faster than you scan thumbnails.** From three metres a row of 16:9 cards is a row of small
pictures you have to decode one by one; a list of six names in 26 px Outfit (52 px on the panel, a capital letter about
23 mm tall) reads in one look, the way a cinema's marquee does from across the street. Each name carries its reason
in the same breath (*New this week*, *Up next*, *A film for tonight*, *On now*), so there is no "Recommended for you"
to work out. Six lines is the number a person can hold at once without counting.

**One picture, not many.** TVs are for pictures, and a 55-inch OLED makes a full-screen backdrop look like a poster
on a wall. Nothing else on the screen competes with it; the lineup sits on a dark scrim at the left, the picture is
clean at the right. Every other direction here, and every streaming home, shares the screen between several pictures
and so shows each one small.

**One focus, one axis.** There is exactly one lit thing on screen at any time (the focused line, in its white ring),
and the main motion is one key: Down. Up / Down walks the lineup, OK does the one obvious thing, Right opens "more of
this". The eye lands on the lit line first (it is the only bright text block), then on the picture that explains it.
A D-pad is best at one axis; grids make people hunt.

**Muscle memory.** The lineup has a fixed order and never reorders while you look at it: continue, new, up next, a
film, the game, on now. After a week the thumb knows "three Downs is the film" without reading. Empty lines are left
out (no "nothing new" placeholders), so the list only ever shrinks from the bottom.

**It knows the clock.** *Ends 21:03*, *ends 23:33*, *until 20:55*, *Thu 02:00*: the one fact that decides what you
watch on a weeknight is when it ends, and Home prints it on every line instead of making you add up runtimes.

**4,812 films and 1,210 series.** Home does not try to show the library; it shows tonight and keeps the library one
press below. The shelves are deliberately few and named by shape, not by algorithm: *A film for tonight* (fits before
midnight, best copy first), *Popular on Cinemeta*, *New this month*, *Á íslensku*, *In 4K*, *Saved*, and
*Everything*, which opens the Movies / Series grid the app already has (six captioned posters a screen, Sort,
Filters, the jump bubble on key repeat). Each shelf shows seven posters at a time with the eighth peeking; the picture
answers the focused poster so a shelf is a slideshow of backdrops, not a strip of thumbnails. The lineup is also where
the library reaches up: the app's daily pick for *A film for tonight* and the next new episode are chosen from the
whole catalog, so the 4,812 are represented by the one the app thinks you want, with its reason printed.

**What others do, and where this is different on purpose.** Netflix: a billboard that auto-plays a trailer over
twenty rows of thumbnails; good for discovery for millions, tiring for one person who knows what he watches, and the
billboard changes under your thumb. Apple TV: a hero carousel and an *Up Next* row, the closest in spirit (one row of
what you were watching), but still a row of small cards with the big art for only the first. Google TV: dense rows,
ads, a "For you" that is mostly not for you. Plex / Infuse / Kodi: continue-first and poster walls; Kodi's skins and
the PlayStation home are the only places a *vertical menu with a picture that answers it* is common, and that is the
pattern the Marquee takes, stripped to six lines and given real art and real logos. The deliberate differences: no
carousel, no autoplay, nothing that moves unless he pressed a key, and the end time on every line.

## 4. Why it's good UI, piece by piece

Callouts 1 to 8 are numbered on `png/case-evening-annotated.png`; 9 and 10 are motion and the library screen.

1. **The top bar** is the app's own, unchanged (mark, Search, tabs, the clock, the account), 40 px tall at y 32–72 so
   its focus ring stays inside the TV's safe area. Kept where it is because every other tab has it there; Home is not
   allowed to be a different app. The clock (*Wed 20:41*) is on the bar because the lineup talks in times.
2. **The lineup** sits at x 48 (the left safe edge), y 92–500, in one column 440 px wide. Six entries at most. The
   unfocused entries are Outfit 700 at 26 px, chalk at 66 % opacity, with the reason and fact in Inter 15 px after the
   name (*New this week · S6 · E6*). One line each, 40 px pitch, so six fit above the safe bottom with room for the
   focused one to grow. Why text and not cards: see section 3; why the left: it is where reading starts and where the
   backdrop's scrim can sit without covering the subject of the picture.
3. **The focused entry** grows to a block (padding 12/18, radius 18) with a 34 % canvas tint behind it so its text has
   its own ground on any picture, and the app's focus ring: 5 px white with a 2 px canvas gap, the same ring as every
   button in airglow, so "this is where I am" means the same thing everywhere. It holds, top to bottom: the eyebrow
   (*CONTINUE WATCHING*, 14 px caps in pink-text: the brand colour as text, never as a fill), the **logo** (Cinemeta's
   own, 46 px tall, chalk when the original is black), the **meta line** (*S2 · E3 · Who Is Alive? · 21 min left · Ends
   21:03* and the 4K badge), the **episode's line** from Cinemeta, the **progress bar** (280 px, the pink-to-orchid
   gradient on a surface-2 track: the one place the gradient is a fill here), and the **hint line** (*OK Resume · ▸
   Episodes · Hold Start over, Details*) in 14 px with the keys drawn as caps. The hint is on the entry, not in a bar
   at the bottom, because it belongs to the thing it is about and is only shown for the lit line.
4. **The picture**: one `<img>` at 960 × 540, `object-fit: cover`, positioned per title so the subject sits in the
   right half. Over it two gradients and nothing else: a left scrim (canvas to clear by x 770) that gives the lineup
   its ground, and a bottom scrim for the footer; a 72 % top scrim behind the bar. The brand's grain sprite is laid
   over at 50 % so the gradients do not band on the OLED. No blur, no video, no mirrored poster.
5. **The logo in place of the name** is the marquee moment. Cinemeta has a logo for almost every title; the app
   already fetches it for the title page. A logo at 46 px is readable because it is a drawn word, and it makes each
   entry look like itself instead of like the app's font. Without a logo, the name stays in Outfit 700 at 38 px (the
   game shows this).
6. **The footer** (*↓ Library · 4,812 films · 1,210 series*, 14 px, bottom right): the honest door to everything else
   and the only hint that Home continues. It sits on the bottom scrim at the right because the left is the lineup's.
7. **Type** is the brand's three faces and nothing else: Outfit 700 for names (it is the wordmark's face), Inter for
   facts and sentences (tabular numbers so *21:03* lines up), Barlow Condensed 800 italic for the clock chip only. No
   text under 14 px except badges (4K, LIVE).
8. **Colour** is the brand's: canvas `#15131D`, chalk, the text tokens, pink-text for eyebrows, the gradient on the
   progress bar, the clock chip and the avatar. Red is LIVE and nothing else. The team colours on game night are the
   teams' real ones, used as data, not as UI: never on a button, a ring or a pill.
9. **Motion** is small and all on opacity and transform: a 200 ms crossfade of the picture and logo on Down / Up, the
   focused block fading its extra lines in. Nothing moves unless he pressed a key; nothing rotates.
10. **The library screen** keeps the same bones: the picture, the left column (eyebrow *↑ TONIGHT · LIBRARY · A FILM
    FOR TONIGHT*, the logo at 56 px, the meta, two lines of plot, the hint), the shelf of 128 × 192 posters along
    the bottom (radius 12, the focused one lifted 10 px and ringed, quality badge top-left, *Ends 23:01* on a
    bottom scrim), and the list of shelves at the right with a pink dot on the current one. The seventh poster peeks
    at the right edge so the row is known to continue.

## 5. Game night

**Why the whole screen.** Game night is one of the few nights the owner opens the app for one specific thing, and the
app should look like it knows. Turning the picture into the two teams' colours is the biggest change Home can make
without changing how it works: the lineup is still the lineup, Down is still Down, OK still plays; only the picture
and the first line changed. The two fields and the single 12° cut are the brand's Sharp dialect, used only here, so
the slant itself means *live*. The crests sit in the brand's neutral disc (a logo is never recoloured), the clock chip
is on the gradient at the cut, the LIVE pills sit on the canvas and on the dark plate, never on a team colour (the
rule from the brand check). A red opponent gets the same clash rule Home v2 uses: the opponent's field goes neutral,
the edges tell the teams apart.

**How the shield keeps him safe.** With the shield on, the screen has no number that could be a score and no record
from after tip-off: the chip is the clock (*2ND 4:12*), the plate says *Score hidden* with the eye-off, the records
under the crests are stamped *as of tip-off*, the fan line is the one computed at tip-off, the NBA tab's pill is LIVE,
not a score, and the other live game is listed by name and channel only. Reveal is two deliberate acts (Hold OK, then
*Reveal Grizzlies at Lakers?*), never a single key. The morning after, the same first line becomes *Last night ·
Grizzlies at Lakers · Replay · Score hidden* until 18:00 (Home v2's cap), and the ordinary lineup returns in the
evening.

## 6. The weak spots, honestly

- **One picture at a time.** He sees six names but only one piece of art; the others are words. Reels and Echo show
  four and six pictures. The fix is not more pictures (that is the thing the Marquee refuses) but the crossfade being
  fast and the next entry's art preloaded, so a Down shows the picture in 200 ms, every time.
- **Six lines is the cap.** A seventh entry (a second new episode, a second game) does not fit above the safe area at
  this size. The rule is one entry per kind (continue, new, up next, a film, the game, on now) and Right on the line for
  the rest of its kind. If he wants more kinds on the first screen, the lines go to 24 px and seven fit.
- **The picture can fight the text.** A backdrop with its subject on the left loses it under the scrim. The app needs
  a focal side per title; Cinemeta does not give one, so the rule is a cheap scan of the 32 px copy (where the
  contrast is) and, failing that, a flat ground in the poster's colour instead of the backdrop.
- **Logos vary.** Some are wide and thin, some are tall; a few are missing. Capped at 46 × 320 px, left-aligned, with
  the 38 px Outfit name as the fallback (the mocks show both).
- **After a month.** The same six lines every night could feel like a form. What keeps it alive is the art (a different
  backdrop every time something changes), the daily film pick, and the end times that are different every evening;
  what must not happen is the list reordering itself to look fresh.
- **Game night is loud.** A full screen of navy and purple at 03:00 is a lot. The lineup side is darkened to the canvas
  so text keeps 4.5:1, and the brightness of the fields can be a setting (*Game night: full / quiet*) with the quiet
  one keeping the colours to the right third.
- **The library is a second layout.** Down past the lineup changes what the bottom of the screen is (a poster shelf).
  It keeps the picture, the column and the ring, so the eye is not lost, but it is one more thing to learn than Reels
  (spin the same reel) or Echo (leaves again). I think the shelf with the picture answering it is worth that.
- **Building it on the Philips.** The WebView is Chrome 113-class on a 2023 TV chip. Risks and fixes: full-screen
  crossfades are two image layers with opacity only (cheap); the growing focused block changes the lineup's height
  (one small layout per press; if it stutters, the extra lines fade in without changing height); backdrops are 1440
  px JPEGs of 150–300 KB each, so the app should ask metahub for the `medium` copy only, keep the six of tonight's
  lineup warm in the HTTP cache, and show the poster's colour as the ground while one loads; the game-night fields
  are plain CSS boxes; no filters, no blur, no box-shadow spreads anywhere.

## 7. Versus the other two

I read `d-reels/IDEA.md`, `f-echo/IDEA.md` and their renders.

**Reels (D).** Four tall reels, one open; Left / Right picks the kind of evening, Up / Down spins the reel.
*Where Reels wins:* the first screen shows four pieces of real art at once, so variety and newness are visible
without a press; browsing never leaves the page (the film reel spins through 212 with a counter); the glow-in-the-room
behind the plates is a stronger, literal use of the brand's name and matches the Ambilight behind his TV. *Where
Marquee wins:* it is one axis, not two (Reels needs "which reel, then which item", and what is inside the New reel
beyond its first item takes a spin to find out); names read faster than four tall crops, and the Reels crops cut
wide art into slices (good for faces, bad for scenes); the full-width backdrop is the more cinematic picture; the
blurred room is a real performance risk on the TV WebView (their own note says it must be a scaled thumbnail, not a
blur); and the Marquee needs less new code (a list and an image, no four-way reflow).

**Echo (F).** Six leaves across the screen; one open at 540 × 540, five folded as 84 px spines with their names
sideways; Right opens the next leaf in place. *Where Echo wins:* everything is on screen as a picture and the chosen
one is still big; the fold is a signature motion the Marquee does not have; Down into a leaf and the library as
leaves again is one idea, where the Marquee has a lineup and then a shelf; the spines carry their own colour (team
navy, the channel's logo, posters) where the Marquee's list is text on a scrim. *Where Marquee wins:* six names in
horizontal 26 px text are read in one look, sideways 22 px names with a 13 px second line are read one at a time
with a head tilt; the picture is the artist's 16:9 frame, not a square cut from it (Echo's own risk list is mostly
about crops); the screen is quieter (one picture and type, against a row of strips of faces that their veil has to
calm); the fold changes the width of six boxes for 320 ms, which is layout on every frame in a WebView, where the
Marquee only fades; and on game night both of us converge on the same graphic (fields, crests, chip), so the
difference is what sits beside it: Echo keeps five spines, the Marquee keeps the rest of the evening as readable
lines under the game.

**Fairly, in one line each.** Reels is the best at showing the evening as art and at browsing in place; Echo is the
best at "everything on screen, one thing big" and has the best motion; the Marquee is the easiest to read, the
easiest to drive with a thumb, the most cinematic single picture, and the cheapest to build well on the TV he has.
