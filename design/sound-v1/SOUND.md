## 0. The short version

- **Character:** soft glass. A sine with a few weak partials, struck, decaying fast. One key (D major pentatonic) so nothing ever clashes. No reverb, no pad wash, no whoosh: the only "space" is a **stepped echo** (discrete repeats, each darker and quieter), which is the sonic version of the three stepped bands. "A signal travels and leaves an afterimage."
- **One grammar:** up a fifth = forward, down a fifth = back, up a third = open, down a third = close, bright with an echo = on, dim without = off.
- **Quiet:** the launch peaks at -20 dBFS and reaches -31 LUFS in its loudest 400 ms, 8 LU under programme level. The alert, the one sound that has to be heard over a game, is -27 LUFS (4 LU under). A focus tick is -21 dBFS peak and about 20 LU under programme. No file peaks above -15.6 dBFS and none is limited (section 4).
- **Focus is nearly subliminal and rate-limited:** a 55 ms tick, one per key press, and while a key is held one soft tick about every 150 ms (a 125 ms gate; never every 50 ms).
- **Not in the player.** No navigation sound over video. The one exception is the "your team is live" alert, which is opt-in already (Account > "Tell me when my team is live").
- **Setting:** one Account row, "Sounds: On / Quiet / Off". Default On, unless Android's own "System sounds" is off, in which case it starts Off until the owner chooses.

## 1. Concept: how the brand sounds

**The brand in one line** (BRAND.md v5.4): *a signal travels through the air (the wedge) and leaves a lit afterimage behind it (the stepped bands); calm by default, sharp only for live sport.* The brand is also defined by what it refuses: no glow, no blur, no soft shadow, no shimmer. Separation comes from steps.

So the sound has to:

1. **Be stepped, not smeared.** A reverb tail is the audio equivalent of a glow; it is also the thing that makes UI sounds sound "app-store generic". airglow's only echo is two discrete repeats at three times the step, each one lower and lowpassed (like the bands at 70 / 50 / 30 %) and each replacing the one before it, so they read as steps. In the launch the three bands literally step out as three notes at the same millisecond the CSS starts them (100, 190, 280 ms).
2. **Be calm and confident.** Soft attacks (2 to 18 ms raised cosine for struck notes, never a hard edge), natural exponential decay, no sustained tones in navigation, nothing in the top octave. Quiet, not timid.
3. **Have a Sharp dialect.** The brand has one identity with two angles (8 degrees soft, 12 degrees live). The sound does the same: the "team is live" alert is the launch's three-step staircase, but an octave up, tighter (3 ms attacks, a short 100 ms decay, no swell) and brighter (stronger upper partials). Same notes, different edge. Soft sounds never get the bright timbre.
4. **Be one family.** Every pitch is in D major pentatonic (D E F# A B), every sound is the same glass-like timbre (a sine, a soft octave, a trace of inharmonic "tink", a barely detuned twin at 0.13 for life in the long sounds). Sounds that follow each other in a flow (focus, select, open) therefore never form a wrong interval, which is the Material guidance that related sounds share timbre and melodic material (section 7).

**The grammar** (a user learns it without knowing it):

| Gesture | Interval / shape | Sounds |
|---|---|---|
| Forward, confirm | rising fifth, D5 to A5 | select |
| Back | falling fifth, A5 to D5, darker | back |
| In | a breath rising 600 to 3000 Hz, landing on F#5 | open |
| Out | a breath falling 3000 to 600 Hz, landing on D5 | close |
| On | bright E6, stepped echo (the afterimage) | toggle-on |
| Off | dim B5, darker, no echo | toggle-off |
| Arrival | three stepped notes D5 F#5 A5, then the stacked chord D5 F#5 A5 E6 | launch; the alert uses the staircase an octave up |

**The signature** is the three-step staircase plus the resolving chord: the bands step out as single notes, and when the lockup is whole they sound together as one. It is two gestures (a staircase and a chord) in 2.3 s. That is deliberate: a launch sound heard fifty times needs a recognisable shape, not a melody. The Netflix "ta-dum" is one struck sound with a swell; the PlayStation and Xbox boot sounds are one gesture each (section 7).

**Mono** because: the TV speakers sit a few centimetres apart, soundbars and Google TV downmix unpredictably (phase cancellation turns a wide stereo shimmer into a hollow one), SoundPool and Web Audio both pan mono for free, and nothing in the concept needs width.

## 2. The set

Levels: **peak** = sample peak of the file in dBFS (the 4x oversampled true peak is within 0.1 dB of it for every file). **M-max** = the loudest 400 ms, K-weighted (ITU-R BS.1770), in LUFS. Programme audio sits at about -23 LUFS integrated (EBU R128), so "LU under programme" is M-max + 23.

| File | Length | Peak | M-max | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `launch.wav` | 2300 ms | -20.0 | -31.0 | the signature (default): the staircase and the chord, synced to the launch animation |
| `launch-full.wav` | 2400 ms | -19.0 | -31.0 | option: the same plus a warm swell and a breath |
| `launch-wipe.wav` | 560 ms | -22.5 | -34.0 | the 8 degree slab wipe: soft air |
| `launch-brief.wav` | 1000 ms | -17.2 | -32.0 | reduced-motion launch: just the resolve, played with the fade |
| `focus.wav` | 55 ms | -21.1 | -43.0 | every D-pad move |
| `select.wav` | 150 ms | -21.0 | -37.0 | OK |
| `back.wav` | 150 ms | -22.0 | -38.0 | Back |
| `open.wav` | 160 ms | -21.8 | -38.0 | a sheet or menu opens |
| `close.wav` | 140 ms | -22.5 | -39.0 | a sheet or menu closes (by Back) |
| `toggle-on.wav` | 115 ms | -18.7 | -37.0 | a setting turns on |
| `toggle-off.wav` | 85 ms | -20.7 | -39.0 | a setting turns off |
| `bump.wav` | 75 ms | -18.0 | -42.0 | "can't go further" (optional, off by default) |
| `alert.wav` | 1000 ms | -15.6 | -27.0 | "your team is live" |

Navigation sounds are at most 160 ms (focus 55 ms, toggles under 120 ms; select, back, open and close are 140 to 160 ms because a gesture needs about 60 ms between two notes to be heard as a gesture and not a blip). Each is one or two struck glass notes (or a breath landing on a note) with at most one echo.

### 2.1 launch (2300 ms, the default), synced to `launch.css` / `launch.js`

The sound starts in the same frame as the `l-run` class (that is `begin()` in `launch.js`, through `sfx.launch(t0)`). Times are from that moment. **Only the two gestures that carry the identity** are left in the default, because it is heard on every cold start (and a TV that kills background apps cold-starts it often).

| ms | Visual (shipped CSS) | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 100 / 190 / 280 | the three bands step out of the wedge (560 ms each, ease-out) | **D5, F#5, A5**, glass, 7 ms soft attack, 160 ms decay. The staircase rises as the bands fan out. |
| 370 to 820 | the wedge moves into the lockup slot (700 to 1400), the letters begin (880) | the staircase **repeats twice, quieter and darker** (30 % then 13 %, lowpassed at 4.2 then 3 kHz), at +270 ms and +540 ms: three times the 90 ms band spacing, so the repeats form two more evenly spaced staircases (onsets every 90 ms from 100 to 820 ms) instead of landing on the next band. Each repeat **replaces** the one before (a 5 ms choke), so you hear steps, not a tail. Then a deliberate gap: tension. |
| 1520 | the last letter is about 80 % in; the lockup reads as whole | the **resolve**: D5 F#5 A5 E6 struck together (12 ms between voices so it blooms rather than clacks), one short 140 ms decay, two darker repeats at +240 and +480 ms (30 %, 13 %), each replacing the last. This is the moment the audience remembers. |
| 2000 | the 8 degree slab starts to wipe | `launch-wipe.wav` (separate file, 2.2). The launch file itself ends at 2300 ms (last repeat of the chord) at exactly zero. |

Why the resolve is at 1520 ms and not 1720 ms: the cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1) ease puts a letter at about 80 % opacity after a third of its 420 ms, so the lockup is visually complete well before its last frame. A sound that lands at the visual finish feels late. I checked this against the real animation in the preview.

**Sync in code:** `sfx.launch(t0)` waits (at most 400 ms) for the file to decode, then starts it with an offset of "time already elapsed since t0 + the context's output and base latency", capped at 280 ms. The first 100 ms of the file are silent, so up to about 100 ms of lateness costs nothing; beyond that it eats into the first note rather than shifting everything late.

**Option: `launch-full.wav`** adds a warm swell under the wedge (D4 + A4, 0.6 to 1.45 s) and a breath of rising air under the letters (0.86 to 1.66 s). The review found both generic or nearly inaudible for a sound heard on every start (the breath was 0.3 % of the energy in v1; in the option it is boosted to 2.8 %), so they are out of the default. To use it, play `launch-full` instead of `launch`; nothing else changes.

**Held launch:** if Home is not ready at 2000 ms, `launch.js` holds the lockup with the bands breathing. The launch sound simply ends in silence (it has already resolved), and `launch-wipe` plays when the wipe really starts. Do not loop anything and do not add a "waiting" sound.

**Soundbar wake:** HDMI-ARC soundbars and some TVs open their audio path only after the first audio arrives and can swallow the first 300 to 800 ms. The design survives that: the resolve chord at 1.5 s is the part that matters, and the staircase is the lead-in. If the owner's Philips clips the staircase, nothing needs to change, and a clipped launch still sounds intentional.

### 2.2 launch-wipe (560 ms)

Band-passed noise sweeping 650 Hz to 3.8 kHz with a bell envelope that peaks at 55 % (the wipe easing is slow-fast-slow), plus a very quiet sine climbing A4 to D6. Air, not a swoosh: it tops out around 4 kHz, so there is no sibilance. Plays when `l-wiping` is added (about 2000 ms normally, later on a held launch). Not played in reduced motion.

### 2.3 launch-brief (1000 ms)

For `prefers-reduced-motion`, where the visual is the wedge alone and then a 120 ms fade: only the resolve chord, with a slower attack (18 ms). **Play it in `finish()` together with `l-fading`**, not at `begin()`: that is the only moment anything happens on screen (v1 had it at start, which sounded a chord over a static wedge for 2 s). Anyone who asked the TV for less motion should not get the full choreography; if the owner wants it silent in that mode too, drop it.

### 2.4 focus (55 ms), the one that decides whether the app feels premium or irritating

A fingertip on glass. A5 (880 Hz), a nearly pure sine (the octave partial is 0.08, was 0.18 in v1), 1.8 ms attack, 10 ms decay constant, plus a 3 ms band-passed noise grain at 0.18 of the tone's amplitude (was 0.07) so it has a physical "touch" and not a pitched pip. Mid-register, nothing above 4 kHz, nothing below 200 Hz. M-max -43 LUFS: by design it should vanish under dialogue and be clear in a quiet pause, which is where "nearly subliminal" should land (to be confirmed on the TV).

**When it plays:** only when a D-pad key press moved focus (not when focus is set by code: a sheet taking focus, a screen loading, Back restoring the opener, data arriving). See "Held keys" for the repeat policy and the direction pitches.

### 2.5 select (150 ms) and back (150 ms)

- select: D5 then A5, **60 ms apart** (a rising fifth), one soft echo on the last note (20 %, +45 ms), plus the touch grain. "Forward."
- back: A5 then D5, 62 ms apart (a falling fifth), lowpassed at 4.2 kHz, no echo, slightly darker and quieter. "Back out."

They are mirror images on purpose, so the pair teaches itself: up means forward, down means back. (v1 had the notes 36 to 42 ms apart, too close to hear as two notes; the review measured that up versus down was audible and the interval was not.) select is 6 dB louder than focus (M-max -37 vs -43) so a commit is distinguishable from a move even when both are quiet.

### 2.6 open (160 ms) and close (140 ms)

Breath-led, so they sound different from select and back and not like a longer version of them (in v1 the breath was 2 to 9 % of the energy; now it is 36 to 37 %).

- open: a 115 ms breath rising 600 to 3000 Hz, with F#5 landing at 90 ms. "In."
- close: a 95 ms breath falling 3000 to 600 Hz, with D5 landing at 70 ms, a little darker. "Out."

They match the sheet's 320 ms translateX(24 px) + fade: the sound is over in about 150 ms, so it marks the start of the motion rather than trying to cover it.

### 2.7 toggle-on (115 ms) and toggle-off (85 ms)

- on: E6 (1318 Hz) with a stepped echo at +50 ms and A5 underneath: **lit**.
- off: B5 (988 Hz), lowpassed at 3 kHz, no echo, shorter: **dim**.

These stand for the toggle's own visual (gradient track and knob on the right, flat track on the left). Used only for Account rows that toggle (Live previews, Score on picture, Your team first, Tell me when my team is live).

### 2.8 bump (75 ms), optional

A soft, dull knock: E4 (330 Hz) with a 4-semitone downward bend over 12 ms, a little noise, lowpassed at 3.2 kHz, M-max -42. **Recommendation: ship the file, leave it off.** In this app edges stop all the time (rails, grids, the top bar) and a user mashing a direction will hit one constantly; a sound there reads as an error, and the brand says errors are neutral. Turn it on only after a trial on the TV if the owner misses an edge cue. The player switch is `sfx.bumpEnabled(true)`.

### 2.9 alert (1000 ms), "your team is live"

The launch staircase **an octave up** (D6, F#6, A6 at 95 ms steps, E7 on top of the last note), in the Sharp dialect: 3 ms attacks, a short 100 ms decay, stronger upper partials, no swell, the repeats at +285 and +570 ms (3 times the step, 28 % and 12 %, each replacing the last). It is the only sound that is allowed over programme audio, so it is the only one tuned against programme: **M-max -27 LUFS** (4 LU under -23; v1 was -29), peak -15.6 dBFS. The review measured v1's alert at 87 % of its energy between 300 Hz and 1 kHz, which is exactly where commentary and crowd noise live, and at -29 it would have been masked under a loud stream. Now 0 % of its energy is below 1 kHz (spectral centroid 1.7 kHz, 92 % between 0.5 and 2 kHz, 8 % between 2 and 6 kHz), so it sits above the speech band, and it is an octave from the launch staircase so the two are not confused. It is also in the ear's most sensitive region, so audition it first for harshness (SOUND.md, Open questions); `m`, `lp` and the BRIGHT partials are the three knobs.

## 3. When a sound plays, and when it must not

### 3.1 Held keys (the focus sound's rules)

A Google TV remote repeats about every 50 ms after a 400 ms delay (the UI is designed for roughly that; the delay before the first repeat is device-specific). A tick every 50 ms is a 20 Hz buzz and, with a 55 ms sound, a continuous smear.

| Event | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| New key press that moved focus | play focus. Minimum 60 ms between two ticks, so mashing is clean. |
| Held key (`e.repeat`) | a tick through a **125 ms gate** at **-4.4 dB** (gain 0.6). A remote repeats about every 50 ms, so the gate passes every third repeat (about 150 ms) even when the repeats jitter; a 150 ms gate would limp between 150 and 200 ms. You hear the scroll speed as a steady soft pulse (6 to 8 a second), not a buzz. "Silent on repeats" is a valid alternative; `preview.html` section 4 lets you compare the three. |
| Left/right | the file as is. |
| Up / Down | the tick is A5; **up plays at rate 1.1225 (B5), down at 0.8409 (F#5)**: both stay in D major pentatonic and never make a tritone with select (D5, A5). Vertical moves are information in a rail UI (changing row), and a pitch difference costs nothing. To avoid two identical ticks in a row without going out of tune, each tick gets +/- 0.3 % in rate (5 cents) and +/- 0.7 dB in gain. |
| Key stopped at an edge | silent (bump is off); if on, new presses only, 400 ms minimum gap, never a repeat. |
| Focus crossing a container on a repeat | the app already forbids crossing the top bar on a repeat; nothing to add. |

### 3.2 One key, one sound

OK on a card plays select. OK on a button that opens a sheet must play **open, not select then open**. OK on a toggle plays toggle-on or toggle-off, not select. Back that closes a sheet plays close, not back. The rule is priority within one key press (`sfx.request`): launch 5 and alert 6 are standalone; open/close 4; select/back/toggle 3; focus 1. The highest wins and the others are dropped. `sfx.js` implements it with a microtask, so a click handler that calls `request()` synchronously inside the key handler is covered.

### 3.3 Never

- **The player.** No navigation sound in the full-screen native player, ever (rationale in 3.5). Alert excepted.
- **Repeats** of OK or Back (`e.repeat` already returns early in `main.js`; keep it).
- **Programmatic focus**: opening a sheet, loading a screen, Back restoring the opener, data refreshes, score updates, toasts. "Data never moves focus" is already a UI rule; data also never makes a sound.
- **Typing on the on-screen keyboard** (`.kb` in Search): OK on a letter, Space, Delete, Clear is silent: the letter appearing in the field is the confirmation, and 36 keys typed fast would otherwise be a xylophone. Focus ticks inside `.kb` stay, at half level (-6 dB), because a 6 x 6 grid with no feedback feels dead; set `gain = 0` there instead if the owner wants the keyboard fully silent. The system TV keyboard (First launch fields) is Android's own and plays Android's sounds.
- **Back that leaves the app** (`native.exit()`): silent.
- **While the launch layer is up**: keys are ignored anyway (`launchOpen()`), so no navigation sound can fire.
- **When the app is hidden** (`document.hidden`) or its Web Audio context is suspended: drop the sound, do not queue it (a suspended context would release a burst of ticks later).
- **Warm resume**: the launch layer is skipped, so no launch sound.
- **The 12 degree live wipe (280 ms)**: stays silent. It is an in-page transition; a sound on top of the action chip's alert and the content change is too much. Revisit only after trying the set on the TV.
- **Search results updating, spoiler-shield reveals, sync results, reconnect and error states**: silent. Errors are neutral by brand rule, so they are neutral in sound too.

### 3.4 Where the launch sound plays

Cold start only, at `begin()` in `launch.js` (the same line that adds `l-run`), after fonts are ready, which is also when the wedge starts to move. If the setting is Off, nothing plays and nothing else changes.

### 3.5 Sounds in the full-screen player: recommendation

**Default: no UI sounds in the player, except the alert.** Reasons: (1) the programme audio is the content, and the user is watching, often a live NBA game with commentary and a crowd; any tick over it is an intrusion and the louder the content, the more the tick would have to be raised to be audible, which is exactly wrong; (2) the player UI (banner, mini guide, panels, hint bar) is mostly keyed: ▲▼ zaps channels (the channel change itself is the feedback), ◀▶ seeks and scrubs on hold, so dozens of key presses per minute would each tick; (3) Media3 plays with audio focus and the player must never duck or be interrupted by a UI sound; (4) the player already has strong visual feedback (banner, zap card, ring). The **alert** is the only deliberate interruption (the owner asked to be told), so it is the only sound that stays, subject to "Tell me when my team is live" being on and Sounds not being Off, and it plays at 0.5 gain when Sounds is Quiet.

Alert rules (UI-SPEC section 7 already says the chip is never shown in the first 10 s after a tune and lasts 8 s): sound once when the chip appears, never repeated, never while another panel is open (the chip does not appear then either), no sound on dismiss.

If the owner later wants an "in-player ticks" option, make it a separate row, default off, and still keep ticks out of seek and zap.

## 4. Loudness, peaks and the technical spec

### 4.1 Targets

TV programme loudness is normalised to about -23 LUFS (EBU R128), with sample peaks near full scale. A UI sound that is audible over -23 LUFS dialogue is a UI sound that is too loud in a quiet room, so the set is tuned **below** programme and judged in a quiet room first.

| Class | M-max target | Peak ceiling | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| hero (launch, launch-full, wipe, brief) | -31 to -34 LUFS | -11 dBFS | 8 to 11 LU under programme (it plays with no user action, so it is the quietest hero it can be); no lead-in click |
| alert | -27 | -12 | the one that has to be heard over programme: 4 LU under, an octave above the speech band |
| nav (select, back, open, close, toggles) | -37 to -39 | -12 to -15 | 14 to 16 LU under programme |
| focus, bump | -43, -42 | -14, -15 | about 20 LU under programme |

Because these sounds are short, the K-weighted 400 ms window averages them with silence (a 55 ms tick loses 8.6 dB inside 400 ms). The short-sound loudness figures are therefore "conservative"; that is why the peaks look high (focus peak -21 dBFS for -43 LUFS). No file peaks above -15.6 dBFS (true peak the same within 0.1 dB), so nothing can clip even if the TV adds a level boost, and there is no limiter anywhere in the chain.

Each recipe states its own target (`m`) and ceiling (`ceil`). `synth.js` normalises the file to `m`, then reduces further if `ceil` would be exceeded (it reports `LIMITED`; today none is). To make everything quieter or louder in one go, change the `m` values; to calibrate on the TV, use the user-level gain in `sfx.js` (`LEVEL`), not the files.

| toggle-off | 85 | -20.7 | -20.7 | -32.3 | -39.0 | 11.5 | 989 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 100 | 0.0 | 0 |
| bump | 75 | -18.0 | -18.0 | -34.1 | -42.0 | 16.2 | 376 | 0.0 | 89.7 | 10.3 | 0.0 | 0 |
| alert | 1000 | -15.6 | -15.6 | -32.3 | -27.0 | 16.6 | 1697 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 91.6 | 8.3 | 0 |

Reading it: **energy sits between 500 Hz and 2 kHz** (the alert between 1 and 3 kHz, above the speech band) (where a small TV speaker is flat and a soundbar's dialogue channel works), nothing under 150 Hz (no rumble: a 2nd-order 150 to 200 Hz high-pass on every file), almost nothing above 6 kHz (no harshness or sibilance; a 2nd-order low-pass at 3.2 to 8.5 kHz per sound). The one exception to "warm midrange" is the bump, which is mostly 330 to 700 Hz on purpose (it is a knock) and is the quietest nav sound.

### 4.3 Checks that were run on every file, every run

- **First and last sample are exactly 0**, DC offset below -130 dBFS.
- **No clicks:** the first 2 ms of every file steps by less than -37 dBFS per sample (all attacks are raised-cosine ramps of at least 1.8 ms) and the last 2 ms by less than -90 dBFS (every file ends in a raised-cosine fade to exactly zero: 14 to 300 ms by file). The largest sample-to-sample step in any file is at most 0.33 of its own peak (the wipe, which is noise); band-limited signals cannot exceed about 1.18, so nothing is a discontinuity.
- **Onset** of each file (first sample above 1 % of peak): 0.1 to 0.5 ms for the tonal nav sounds and the alert, so no built-in latency; open builds its breath in 13 ms and close in 2 ms; launch at 101 ms (band 1, as scheduled), launch-wipe at 56 ms.
- **Peak/true peak:** 4x oversampled (32-tap windowed sinc), within 0.1 dB of the sample peak everywhere.
- **No warble:** the chord and staircase tails were checked in 10 ms RMS steps: they decay monotonically, with only the 1 to 3 dB re-strikes of the echo hand-offs (v1 had 15 to 20 dB dips from a 2 to 5 Hz beat).
- **Determinism:** two consecutive runs give identical MD5s for every file (the only randomness is a seeded PRNG for noise grains).
- **Decoded in Chrome** (headless): all thirteen decode through `decodeAudioData`; the preview, its menu and held-key tester run without errors; `sfx.js` was exercised in the same Chrome for the request gate (no key: silent; key down: highest priority wins; a key held 600 ms still opens a hold menu; 400 ms after release: silent), `launch(t0)` offset compensation, `zone('player')` suspending and resuming the context, and a simulated jittery 50 ms repeat for 2 s (7.1 ticks a second).

### 4.4 Format

48 kHz, 16-bit PCM, mono, plain WAV (44-byte header). 48 kHz because Android TV outputs at 48 kHz natively (SoundPool plays it as is; Web Audio resamples on its own if the context runs at another rate, so `sfx.js` leaves the context's rate alone); 16-bit because it is universally decodable and the noise floor (-96 dBFS) is far below the quietest tail we produce; mono for the reasons in section 1. No dither: the files are generated and the fades end at exact zero. Total 796 KB, about 1.6 MB decoded as float: trivially preloadable.

## 5. Why these choices: what separates premium from cheap

From the research below and from taking apart what makes the "cheap" UI sounds irritating:

| Cheap | Premium (what this set does) |
|---|---|
| Pure sine or square beeps, hard onset | Struck-note envelope: 2 to 18 ms soft attack, exponential decay, a little noise "touch" |
| One timbre everywhere, but different pitch for no reason | One timbre family, one key, a grammar of intervals |
| Same sample at the same pitch 20 times a second | One tick per press, about 150 ms spacing on repeats, up to B5 and down to F#5 per direction, 0.3 % and 0.7 dB jitter |
| Energy at 3 to 5 kHz (loudest to the ear, harsh on small speakers) | Spectral centroid 600 to 1300 Hz for the tonal sounds (2.1 kHz for the wipe), almost nothing above 6 kHz |
| A reverb wash to "sound expensive" | Discrete, darkening echoes at three times the step, each replacing the last; a dry body. No slow beating partials. (And no glow, per the brand.) |
| Loud, with a long tail | 4 to 20 LU under programme; navigation 160 ms at most |
| Sound for everything | Sound for moves, commits and layers; silence for typing, data, errors, repeats, and the player |
| Different levels from sound to sound at random | A level ladder: focus < bump < back/open/close < select/toggle < alert/launch |

## 6. Settings

Add one row to Account (the "General" or "Playback" group, next to "Live previews"):

**Sounds**: On, Quiet, Off. A radio menu like the other choice rows (`radioMenu`), not a toggle; the row's right-hand value shows the current choice.
Description: "Soft sounds when you move around. Never in the player, except 'my team is live'."

| Choice | Effect |
|---|---|
| On | files at gain 1.0 |
| Quiet | gain 0.5 (-6 dB) on everything, alert included |
| Off | nothing at all, alert and launch included |

**Default: On, but follow Android once.** Android (on Google TV the switch is under Settings > Display and Sound > Audio > System sounds, path from memory) has a system-wide sound-effects switch (`Settings.System.SOUND_EFFECTS_ENABLED`, "Touch sounds"). Some owners turn it off because the stock clicks irritate, and would be surprised by sound that ignores that. So:

```js
// mode: the user's choice wins; otherwise follow the system setting once
const mode = settings.sounds ?? (native?.systemSoundsEnabled?.() === false ? 'off' : 'on');
sfx.mode(mode);
```

`settings.sounds` is `undefined` until the owner touches the row; from then on the row is the truth, and the system setting is no longer consulted. The bridge call is one line in the native plugin (`Settings.System.getInt(contentResolver, SOUND_EFFECTS_ENABLED, 1) == 1`; I could not verify against the Philips whether its Google TV build toggles the same key, so test: flip System sounds and read it back). In a desktop browser (no bridge) the default is On.

A global volume slider is **not** recommended: the TV's volume is the volume, and three steps (On, Quiet, Off) cover every real situation without a fourth thing to explain. If a quiet-room owner asks for "quieter still", lower `LEVEL.quiet` to 0.35.

Reduced motion does not turn sounds off (different setting, different need). It only selects `launch-brief`.

## 7. Research behind the choices

I searched for primary material and for what I could not find I say so. Where a point below is my own practice and not from a source, it is marked (judgement).

**Startup sounds (hero moments)**
- **Netflix "ta-dum":** commissioned by Todd Yellin from sound editor Lon Bender with the brief "tension, release, quirkiness"; Bender produced 20 to 30 candidates; the winner is reported to be a wedding ring tapped on a nightstand plus a reversed guitar chord ("The Blossom") as the swell; a goat's scream was an early candidate (the MGM lion idea). The accounts differ, and Netflix executives dispute the origin story, so only the structure is relied on: **one struck transient plus one swell, i.e. tension then release**. airglow's version: staircase (tension of rising steps) then chord (release). Sources: [/Film](https://slashfilm.com/netflixs-signature-launch-sound-was-almost-the-bleating-of-a-goat), [Twenty Thousand Hertz episode](https://www.20k.org/episodes/netflix), [Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2020/08/07/ta-dum-how-the-netflix-sound.html/amp), [The Scotsman](https://www.scotsman.com/whats-on/netflix-tudum-sound-origin-explained-5018701).
- **Original Xbox boot sound (Brian Schmidt):** built to cover the hardware's start-up delay, from three tiny elements (thunder, a cannon, the attack of a glockenspiel) downsampled to 6 kHz to fit 25 kB. Lesson: a start-up sound is partly there to fill the wait and should be built from very few, very clear elements; and a distinctive sound can come from a tiny palette. Sources: [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/designing-the-boot-sound-for-the-original-xbox), [CDM](https://cdm.link/read-this-story-of-how-the-original-xbox-startup-sound-was-created/), [GameSoundCon](https://www.gamesoundcon.com/post/creating-the-original-xbox-boot-sound-using-old-school-game-audio-techniques-in-a-modern-console), [20k episode](https://www.20k.org/episodes/xboxstartupsound).
- **Original PlayStation (Takafumi Fujisawa):** designed against the ROM size limit and to fit a ready-made logo animation: the sound serves the picture. Lesson: sound is synced to the logo animation, which is the same job as syncing to the CSS. Source: [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2019/12/05/how-takafumi-fujisawa-created-the-original-playstations-startup-sound/).
- **PS5:** I could find videos of the UI sounds (via [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/ps5-menu-music/)) and the 2025 Audio Focus feature, which is headphone-only and not applied over HDMI to TVs or soundbars ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2025/04/23/new-ps5-system-software-update-features-audio-focus-and-the-return-of-the-classic-console-ui-customizations/)). I found no interview with the PS5 UI sound designers. Not found: so nothing in this set claims to follow Sony's method. (judgement) What is audible in the PS5 and Switch UIs: short, dry, mid-register ticks with a distinct confirm and back, and varied startup sounds that are never the same twice; I used the "short, dry, per-gesture" part.
- **Nintendo Switch, Apple tvOS, Google TV:** I found no primary source on their UI sound design (the one Apple result was a WWDC23 visionOS session that mentions varying repetitive sounds: [WWDC23 10271](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023-10271)). (judgement) tvOS leans nearly silent on focus; Switch pairs a dry per-move tick with a clear confirm; Google TV's own sounds come from Android's system effects. I have not made claims about them beyond that.

**Principles (sources)**
- **Hierarchy, and related sounds share traits:** Material's sound choreography ranks sounds by importance (brand, hero, alerts, primary UX, secondary UX) and says sounds that follow or precede one another should share timbre, melody or envelope. That is the level ladder and the one-key one-timbre rule. [Material sound choreography](https://m2.material.io/design/sound/sound-choreography.html), [About sound](https://m2.material.io/design/sound/about-sound.html).
- **Fatigue and silence:** Material's "Applying sound to UI" says to limit decorative sounds to reduce fatigue and that building silence into a UI is as important as knowing when to apply sound. That is the "sound for moves, commits and layers; silence for the rest" rule and the 150 ms repeat limit. [Applying sound to UI](https://m2.material.io/design/sound/applying-sound-to-ui.html).
- **Earcon research (Brewster et al., Glasgow):** intensity alone is a poor, annoying way to get attention (so the alert is distinguished by shape and rhythm, not by being loud); plain sine and square tones mask poorly (so each note has partials); distinct rhythms and registers aid recognition. [Earcon guidelines](https://www.dcs.gla.ac.uk/~stephen/earcon_guidelines.shtml).
- **Loudness:** EBU R128: programme loudness -23 LUFS, true peak ceiling -1 dBTP; for very short material the integrated figure is not meaningful, so I measure the loudest 400 ms and the true peak. [EBU R128 s1](https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/r/r128s1.pdf), [R128](https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/r/r128v4_0.pdf). No source gives an earcon-specific LUFS target: the targets in 4.1 are my own (judgement), meant to be judged on the real TV.
- **Audio-visual sync (from memory, not re-checked):** ITU-R BT.1359-1 puts the detectability thresholds at about 45 ms audio ahead of picture and 125 ms audio behind. So a late sound (Web Audio on a TV SoC) is tolerated better than an early one, and I place events at or slightly before the picture, never after.
- **Chrome autoplay:** Web Audio is subject to the autoplay policy since Chrome 71: a context created before a user gesture is suspended and needs `resume()` after one. A D-pad press counts as a gesture, the launch does not. [Autoplay policy in Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay).
- **Android:** `SoundPool` is for short sounds loaded into memory; `Settings.System.SOUND_EFFECTS_ENABLED` is the system sound-effects switch; `AudioManager.playSoundEffect` plays Android's own. I could not fetch the Android reference pages in this session, so these are from knowledge and marked "verify" where it matters (section 8).
- **Psychoacoustics (judgement, standard):** the ear is most sensitive at 2 to 5 kHz (so keep ticks lower and quiet there), temporal integration makes sounds under about 100 to 200 ms seem quieter than their energy suggests (so the peak is higher than the LUFS implies), and TV speakers and many soundbars roll off below 150 to 200 Hz (so no content there, and the low swell relies on its upper partials).

## 8. Integration notes for the engineer

### 8.1 Files and naming

Copy `out/*.wav` to `app/tv/sounds/` and `sfx.js` to `app/tv/js/sfx.js`. For native: Android `res/raw` does not allow hyphens, so use `launch_wipe`, `launch_brief`, `toggle_on`, `toggle_off`. Play every file at gain 1.0 times the user level (1.0 On, 0.5 Quiet); do not re-normalise or compress. The loudness calibration is in the files.

### 8.2 Web (WebView, Chrome 113)

- **Preload:** `sfx.init()` at start-up, before `start()`. 13 files, 796 KB; the launch files decode first (`sfx.launch` waits up to 400 ms for its file), the rest in the background. A sound not yet decoded is dropped silently (never queued).
- **Autoplay:** create the context at load (it will be `suspended`), resume on the first key (`sfx.js` does this on `keydown` and `pointerdown` in the capture phase). **The launch plays before any key**, which is fine here: Capacitor already sets `setMediaPlaybackRequiresUserGesture(false)` on its WebView (per the review; not re-checked by me), so the context starts `running`. Still log `sfx.state().ctx` once on the TV; if it ever reads `suspended` at launch, play the launch through the native path (8.3). `sfx.play()` drops sounds while suspended rather than queueing them.
- **Latency:** Web Audio on a TV SoC may add 50 to 150 ms. Per BT.1359-1 a late sound of up to about 125 ms is not detectable by most viewers, which is fine for focus ticks and the launch. Measure it: `sfx.state()` returns `baseLatency` and `outputLatency`. The launch compensates for itself: `sfx.launch(t0)` skips from the start of the file whatever time has elapsed since the animation began plus those two latencies (the first 100 ms of the file are silent, so up to ~100 ms is free). If the sum is over about 100 ms, route the navigation sounds through native `SoundPool` (8.3) too.
- **Do not**: use `<audio>` elements (high latency, one at a time), use HTML5 `playbackRate` tricks other than `AudioBufferSourceNode.playbackRate`, or play with `setTimeout` chains.
- **Chrome flags:** none needed.

### 8.3 Native (SoundPool), for the player's alert and as the low-latency path

```kotlin
val attrs = AudioAttributes.Builder()
    .setUsage(AudioAttributes.USAGE_MEDIA)               // follows the media volume that the programme uses
    .setContentType(AudioAttributes.CONTENT_TYPE_SONIFICATION)
    .build()
val pool = SoundPool.Builder().setMaxStreams(4).setAudioAttributes(attrs).build()
// load all 13 at start; wait for OnLoadCompleteListener before the first play
// play: pool.play(id, level, level, priority, 0, rate)   // level 1.0 / 0.5; rate 1.0 (focus: 0.8409 down, 1.1225 up)
```

- **Never request audio focus** for these sounds (no `AudioFocusRequest`): they must mix on top of the programme without pausing or ducking Media3.
- **Stream choice:** `USAGE_MEDIA` keeps the sounds on the same volume stream as the programme, so a calibration relative to programme stays true as the user changes volume. `USAGE_ASSISTANCE_SONIFICATION` would put them on the system stream, which on a TV may be a different, fixed or unrelated level. I could not verify this on the Philips; test with the TV volume at 10, 30 and 60.
- **Expose** a plugin method, for example `TunerPlayer.sfx({ name, rate, gain })` and `TunerPlayer.systemSounds()`; the web `sfx.js` can call it when `native` exists. The alert is called by native code itself when the chip is shown.
- SoundPool is for short samples held in memory; all thirteen are small (the longest, launch-full, is 230 KB as WAV). Playback `rate` is supported from 0.5 to 2.0.
- Keep the stream warm? Audio HALs may clip the first few ms after a long idle. Check the first focus tick after 10 s idle on the TV; if clipped, do not add a silent loop (power, soundbar wake): lengthen nothing, accept it, or prime with one inaudible play at start.

### 8.4 Wiring (the app's own files; I did not touch them)

`main.js`, in the key handler:

```js
import { sfx } from './sfx.js';
sfx.init(); sfx.mode(settings.sounds ?? (native?.systemSoundsEnabled?.() === false ? 'off' : 'on'));
app.addEventListener('tvfocus', (e) => sfx.focused(e.target));       // every focus change; sfx ignores the ones that were not a key press

// in the keydown handler, where DIRS is handled:
if (DIRS.has(k)) { sfx.dir(k, e.repeat); const moved = move(k, { repeat: e.repeat }); if (!moved) sfx.bump(e.repeat); return; }
// (screens with their own key() handler that return true skip this; their focus changes still tick through 'tvfocus')

// OK: in okPress(), before el.click():
if (!el.closest('.kb')) sfx.request('select');                        // typing is silent; anything higher requested by the click wins
// Back: in back(). closeTop() itself requests 'close' (see ui.js below); every other branch that leaves something requests 'back':
if (closeTop()) return;
...  if (nav.stack.length) { sfx.request('back'); pop(); return; }
...  // the "scroll to top and focus the tab" branch and "go Home": sfx.request('back');   native.exit(): nothing
```

`ui.js`: `openOverlay()` calls `sfx.request('open')` (once per call); **`closeTop()`** (the Back path) calls `sfx.request('close')`; **not `closeOverlay()`**, because a pick that closes a menu is an OK (select), not a Back. `request()` ignores a call that no key caused (nothing down, nothing released in the last 300 ms), so an overlay opened by code, such as an update prompt, never chimes.

`screens/account.js`: after a toggle, `sfx.request(on ? 'toggle-on' : 'toggle-off')`.

`launch.js`:

```js
function begin() {
  t0 = performance.now();
  if (reduce) app.classList.add('l-rm');                       // the wedge alone: no sound yet
  else { sfx.launch(t0); app.classList.add('l-run'); }         // skips the time already elapsed + output latency; 'launch-full' for the long one
  ...
}
function finish() {
  ...
  if (reduce) { sfx.play('launch-brief'); app.classList.add('l-fading'); ... return; }   // the chord lands with the 120 ms fade
  sfx.play('launch-wipe'); app.classList.add('l-wiping'); ...
}
```

Native player: when the native player opens call `sfx.zone('player')`, when it closes `sfx.zone('ui')`. The WebView stays awake under the player, so this suspends the context and nothing can chime under it; the alert is played by native code (8.3).

### 8.5 Rate limits (also in `out/manifest.json`)

| Sound | Priority | Minimum gap | Held / repeat |
|---|---|---|---|
| focus | 1 | 60 ms | repeats: a 125 ms gate (about 150 ms) at gain 0.6; up = rate 1.1225, down = 0.8409 |
| bump | 1 | 400 ms | never on repeat |
| select, back, toggle-on/off | 3 | 120 to 150 ms | repeats ignored |
| open, close | 4 | 200 ms | |
| launch-wipe, launch-brief | 4 | - | once (brief in `finish()` with `l-fading`) |
| launch, launch-full | 5 | - | once, cold start only |
| alert | 6 | 3 s | once per chip, from native code |

### 8.6 Test checklist for the TV

1. Programme audio on, volume where dialogue is comfortable. Trigger focus ticks: you should be able to hear them in a quiet pause and not under speech. If you can hear them over speech, lower `focus.m` by 3 and re-run; if you cannot hear them at all in a quiet room, raise by 3.
2. Launch at that same volume: it must not make you reach for the remote. If it does, lower `launch.m`.
3. Hold right on a long rail for 3 s: a soft pulse, never a buzz.
4. Search keyboard: typing silent, moves ticking at half level.
5. Open an Options sheet with OK: one sound (open), not two.
6. Back out of a sheet: close. Back from a page: back. Back at Home: silence.
7. Play a game, wait for the chip: alert once, under the commentary but audible.
8. Cold start with the soundbar in standby: the chord should land even if the staircase is clipped.
9. Turn Android System sounds off with Sounds never touched: silent. Set Sounds On in the app: sounds.
10. Check `sfx.state()`: `ctx` is `running` at launch? `outputLatency` under about 100 ms?

### 8.7 Changing a sound

Edit its entry in `RECIPES` at the top of `synth.js`: `f` (note), `att`/`tau` (attack and decay), `partials` (timbre), `echo` (the afterimage), `m` (loudness target), `ceil` (peak ceiling). Run `node design/sound-v1/synth.js` and read the table it prints; open `preview.html` to listen. The notes table (`NOTE`) is D major pentatonic: keep to it.
