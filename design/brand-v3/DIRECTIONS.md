# Tuner v3: four directions

A concept-level restart, not a polish pass. The owner's reaction to v2.2 ("doesn't scream TV and shows and movies watching awesomeness") was that the amber T-with-a-seam read as a generic letter mark or a hardware brand. Nothing from v1–v2.2 was protected: the name, the needle idea and the palette are all open here. See `directions.png` (all four side by side), `<name>-tv.png` (the 1080p home mock for each), `NAMES.md` (the name shortlist and clash checks).

Rules held in every direction (from the reviewer and the owner): **red only for LIVE** (the same `#FF4B4B` pill in all four); errors are neutral (✕ + text, none shown red); **no amber or yellow** anywhere (IMDb, Plex and DAZN own it; the reviewer measured v2.2's amber at ΔE 2.9 from IMDb); the **mark and the full lockup never share a screen** (the rail carries the mark, the lockup lives on splash, banner and sign-in); one focus at a time on screen; 5% safe margins.

## What the research says carries the feeling

- **One gesture, one motion, one sound.** Netflix's folded ribbon is paired with the "tudum" sting and a ribbon-rush animation ([Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/news/netflix-channels-the-magic-of-the-cinema-in-new-ident), [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/best-designs/logo/netflix-n-logo)); Disney+'s arc leads the eye to the "+" and brightens as it nears it; Paramount+ is a mountain with a ring of stars; Peacock leans into fluttering motion and a whoosh ([Fable: streamer logo animations](https://www.fable.app/blog/top-5-streamer-logo-animations), [NewscastStudio](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2020/09/15/cbs-all-access-paramount-plus)). The mark is a *moment*, not a static letter. (This is why v2.2 failed: a static letter with no motion, light or play.)
- **Light, not just colour.** Cinema marquees are bulb grids in white, yellow, red and blue ([Design Museum of Chicago](https://www.designchicago.org/s/theater_marquee_project.pdf), [Wagner Sign catalog](https://wagnersign.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Marquee_Catalog.pdf)); Netflix's real marquee billboard became a campaign in itself ([The One Show](https://creativepool.com/the-one-show/projects/the-netflix-marquee-for-netflix)). On a dark TV, glow, spotlight and bloom are what "screen" feels like.
- **Colour energy needs a free hue.** Streamers have clustered on blue and purple ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/?p=1167922), [Wix](https://www.wix.com/studio/blog/streaming-blue-branding)); red, green and yellow/amber are held by Netflix, Hulu, and IMDb/Plex (Plex also uses accent tokens that Plex users can swap, [Plex](https://plex.tv/blog/finding-your-true-colors-with-plex)). Open: volt lime, orchid, teal/magenta.
- **Sports and broadcast language.** ESPN codified a fixed angle and a custom face (v1 research, [Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2026/03/09/espn-brand-identity)); DAZN's identity is a modular line motif with energetic motion graphics ([Cannes Lions entry](https://lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/coprorate-identity-35328), [Fanatic](https://fanatic.co.uk/work/dazn)); the NBA app moved to social-style vertical video ([sportsvideo.org](https://www.sportsvideo.org/?p=195624)); the lower-third/chyron, on-air bug and tally are the grammar of "live".
- **Classic TV artefacts are already iconic.** SMPTE colour bars ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMPTE_color_bars)), the film leader's countdown and slate with crosshair ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_leader), [Avid wiki](https://www.avid.wiki/SMPTE_Universal_Leader)).
- **A couch UI.** Larger, bolder elements, clear single focus, 5% safe margins, fewer components ([Smashing](https://smashingmagazine.com/2025/09/designing-tv-principles-patterns-practical-guidance/), [Solid Digital](https://www.soliddigital.com/blog/designing-10-foot-user-experience)); a Google TV app is mostly content art, so the chrome should be bold, simple and quiet where the artwork is loud.
- **Gaps in my research:** I could not find usable primary sources for HBO Max, Apple TV, Prime Video, Hulu, NBA League Pass or ESPN branding beyond the above; those brands informed the colour-field map (red, green, yellow, blue taken) but not specifics. Treat brand colour values (`#F5C518` IMDb, `#E5A00D` Plex, and the rest on the board) as reported, not official.

## The four directions

### 1 · SLATE: cinema, title card (name: SLATE)
- **Idea:** every night is take one: the clapper snaps shut and the screen lights up.
- **Mark:** a white clapperboard with a diagonal-striped stick opened at 9° and an orchid play triangle on the board. **Lockup:** mark + SLATE in heavy high-contrast serif (Playfair Display Black, outlined). **Palette:** ink `#0C0B12`, chalk `#F3EFE6`, neon orchid `#E04BFF`.
- **Icon:** ink tile, glowing clapper. **Banner:** ink with a spotlight cone and the lockup. **Home mock:** serif headings, an orchid spotlight cone on the hero, marquee-bulb dots in the eyebrow, orchid glow on focus.
- **Launch:** dark; a spotlight cone fades up; the clapper snaps shut with a clack; the play triangle flickers on like neon (3 flickers); SLATE wipes in under the beam; the beam widens into home. 2.0 s, clack plus a low swell.
- **Strengths:** the most "movies" of the four and the cleanest icon silhouette; neon plus spotlight is real cinema vocabulary; the chalk-white mark is high contrast on dark, where v2.2's amber and white tones fought each other; the name is one hard syllable and means both film and broadcast.
- **Risks:** a clapperboard is the cliché movie glyph; sports energy has to come from the content; Playfair's hairlines blur at distance (checked in `small` renders: SLATE letters soften first); two real apps already use "Slate" for movies.
- **Icon size / across the room:** at 96 and 48 px the clapper reads; at **24 px** the stripes fuse into a grey band and the mark becomes a white box with a triangle, so it needs a simplified small version. Across the room the white mark carries; the neon glow only helps in the splash, not in the rail.

### 2 · TIPOFF: live sports, broadcast energy (name: TIPOFF)
- **Idea:** game-night energy: a slanted wedge and an italic T that is already moving before you press play.
- **Mark:** an italic heavy T with three speed streaks. **Lockup:** TIPOFF in Barlow Condensed ExtraBold Italic, the T in volt, a slanted underline. **Palette:** deep navy `#070B1B`, volt `#C6FF3D`, white `#F4F7FF`.
- **Icon:** a volt tile with a navy T. **Banner:** navy with a volt wedge wipe and the lockup. **Home mock:** italic condensed caps, a volt 14° wedge in the hero, skewed tiles, skewed volt primary button, large italic score.
- **Launch:** hard cut to navy; a volt wedge wipes in at 12° in three frames; TIPOFF slams in from the left, the T lands first with a 2-frame shake; streaks fly past; the wedge sweeps off to reveal home. 1.6 s, court squeak plus a hit.
- **Strengths:** the most energy, and the volt hue is empty (ΔE ≥ 15 from every reference brand colour; clearly not IMDb's yellow); the italic T survives 24 px with streaks intact; angle + wipe is exactly the broadcast-graphics language; it is the best fit for live NBA, the app's main job.
- **Risks:** the name boxes the app as sports (movies and shows feel off-name); a full volt tile is loud in a dark room (unverified on the real TV); italics tire in long text (limited to headings and numerals).
- **Icon size / across the room:** the best of the four at both: the lime tile jumps out from a row of dark tiles, the italic T is legible at 24 px, and the wedge plus italic caps read from far away.

### 3 · airtime: streaming, colour as light (name: airtime)
- **Idea:** time on air: a glowing play button that is also an A, with colour behaving like light.
- **Mark:** a rounded upward triangle (an A) in a teal → blue → magenta gradient with a play-triangle cut-out. **Lockup:** mark + lowercase "airtime" in Outfit Bold. **Palette:** teal `#1FE0C8`, periwinkle `#5B7CFA`, magenta `#FF3FA4` on ink `#090A1F`.
- **Icon:** a gradient tile with the white mark. **Banner:** ink with three blurred colour blooms. **Home mock:** big rounded corners, a gradient primary button, gradient progress bars and glowing focus, a faint triangle in the hero.
- **Launch:** a soft bloom opens from the centre; the triangle draws on along its gradient; the play cut-out pops; colour trails sweep outward like light through a prism; "airtime" rises in; the gradient floods and dissolves into home. 2.2 s, rising shimmer and a warm chord.
- **Strengths:** the play affordance is explicit and friendly; glow plus gradient says "screen" and "now" at once; the cut-out mark is simple enough to animate (the draw-on is the star).
- **Risks:** the least ownable palette (blue, violet and pink gradients are the streaming default; the periwinkle is only 9.1 ΔE from Paramount+'s blue, so the middle of the gradient is the weak spot); the name clashes moderately with the Airtime group-watch app; lowercase is soft for a sports night.
- **Icon size / across the room:** the gradient tile is distinct and reads at 48 px; at 24 px the play cut-out almost disappears, leaving a white triangle. Across the room: fine, bright, friendly; the gradient text on the hero loses punch against the busy bloom.

### 4 · TUNER: test card, broadcast heritage (name: TUNER, kept)
- **Idea:** own the engineering: the colour-bar test card, as party, becomes the T.
- **Mark:** a T whose crossbar is five colour bars (white, cyan, lime, magenta, periwinkle) over a paper-white stem. **Lockup:** the T plus UNER in Space Grotesk Bold. **Palette:** ink `#0E1014`, paper `#F3F0E8`, signal aqua `#00D8F0` (bars as the playful secondary set).
- **Icon:** the bars run full-bleed across the top of the tile, the stem drops from them. **Banner:** ink with a bar strip at the top and bottom edges and the lockup. **Home mock:** square-ish corners, a bar strip on the hero and under each section header, a bar-colour edge on every channel tile, a hard-shadow button.
- **Launch:** black; a thin horizontal line opens with a 1 kHz "pip" into a five-bar test card (0.4 s); the bars collapse into the T's crossbar, the stem drops in, UNER types on; the card settles into home. 1.8 s, test tone cut to a chord.
- **Strengths:** keeps the name the owner called clean and uses its "engineering" quality as the joke; a test card is instantly "TV" and the bars become a flexible system (dividers, tile edges, idle screen); the full-bleed icon is the most visible of the dark-tile family.
- **Risks:** the most playful and the least "awesome" of the four, nothing cinematic and nothing about sports; the five-colour crossbar gets busy at 16 px; the name still reads technical; aqua is 7.9 ΔE from Letterboxd's blue (flagged on the board), lime is 10.8 from Hulu.
- **Icon size / across the room:** the full-bleed bars make it pop in a launcher row; at 24 px it holds as a colourful T. Across the room the bars carry; the paper stem alone is plain.

## How they compare

| | SLATE | TIPOFF | airtime | TUNER |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Says movies | strongest | weak | medium | medium (test card) |
| Says live sports | weak | strongest | medium | weak |
| Says "TV" | medium | medium | medium | strongest |
| Energy / "awesome" | high (bold, cinematic) | highest | high, soft | playful, lowest |
| Icon at 24 px | weakest (stripes fuse) | best | cut-out dies | holds |
| Ownable palette | orchid, yes | volt, yes | gradient, weakest | bars, yes |
| Name risk | moderate clash | low clash, boxes sport | moderate clash | none, but reads technical |

**My read:** TIPOFF is the best answer to "screams live NBA"; SLATE is the best answer to "movies and shows"; if only one has to carry all three jobs (live sports, movies, series) at once, SLATE with the red LIVE pill reads the broadest, and TIPOFF is the boldest bet. A real option is to take TIPOFF's colour and wedge energy into SLATE (volt wedge motion with the clapper mark). Not built; that is a pick-then-polish step.

## What this is not

No tokens, no full system, no final logo files: marks, lockups, icons and banners are concept renders for choosing. The launch animations are described, not built. Nothing was tested on the real Philips panel; screens are rendered at 1920×1080 and judged by eye plus blur/small-size tests.

## Rebuild

`tools/`: `make-wordmarks.js` (one-off, outlines the four wordmarks into `wordmarks.json` from the @fontsource files), `assets.js` + `write-assets.js` (mark, lockup, icon, banner SVGs per direction), `mock.js` (the four TV home pages), `board.js` (directions.html), `render.js` (Playwright), `color.js` (ΔE). Fonts used (all SIL OFL, `fonts/`): Playfair Display, Barlow Condensed, Outfit, Space Grotesk, Inter.
`node tools/write-assets.js && node tools/mock.js && node tools/board.js`, then `node tools/render.js <page>.html <png> w h`.
